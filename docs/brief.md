# Brief

## One line

A portfolio template for a product designer that makes senior-level strength obvious within seconds, shows the bottom line of each project to anyone, and saves the full story for in person.

## Audience

- Design leaders and managers.
- Hiring managers and recruiters, often scanning on a phone or from a shared link.
- Cross-functional peers (product, engineering, research) who are not designers.

Each of them should get the point without being walked through it.

## Goals

1. **Showcase senior-level strength through evidence, not claims.** No self-praise, and nothing framed around career level, titles or job moves.
2. **Teaser depth.** Each case reads in about 30 seconds and at most 2 screens. The depth is implied, and told in person.
3. **Modern, classy, cool.** It must not look like a template. Style comes from the taste skills in `.agents/skills/`, picked from generated concepts (Phase 0), not defaults.
4. **Controlled sharing.** Case studies are password gated; the bottom line stays public.
5. **Reusable.** All copy and media live in `content/` and `public/`, so swapping the placeholders is file edits, not layout work.

## How strength shows

- **Impact:** 2 to 3 metrics per case, each with a baseline or time window so it is credible.
- **Scope:** scope chips, team size and partners show the size of the problems.
- **Range:** the 4 cases each carry a different senior signal:
  - Design system adoption at scale (leverage, influence without authority).
  - Core workflow redesign (measurable efficiency, hard tradeoffs).
  - 0 to 1 product bet (ambiguity, research to launch, business framing).
  - Cross-org accessibility program (multi-team leadership, standards).
- **Craft:** large real artifacts, and the site itself as proof of craft.
- **Judgment:** strict brevity, plus "Ask me about" prompts that point to depth without writing it out.
- **Discretion:** gated work reads as real and sensitive, shared on purpose.

## Avoid

Self-praise adjectives, testimonial carousels, award walls, skill bars, tool logo grids, long process essays, "lessons learned" walls.
